Time Dilation — The Light Clock

A light clock is a pair of mirrors a distance L apart with a photon bouncing between them. One "tick" = one round trip.

  • In its rest frame (top): the photon goes straight up and back. Round trip time: τ = 2L/c.
  • In a frame where the clock moves at v (bottom): the photon traces a longer zigzag path of length 2 √(L² + (vT/2)²). Since light still travels at c, the round-trip time T satisfies cT = 2 √(L² + (vT/2)²), giving T = γ τ where γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²).

Result: moving clocks run slow by a factor γ. This is not an illusion or a defect of the clock — it is the geometry of spacetime. Any other clock (mechanical, atomic, biological) shows the same dilation.
